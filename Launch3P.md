Program Overview

The RFID 3P TD Launch Training Program transforms our current RFID internal blue badge onsite store launch process into a streamlined, scalable 3P vendor-owned model supporting 300% YoY growth: 150 store launches in 2026 (~12-15 simultaneous launches monthly) and 450 in 2027 (~30-37 simultaneous launches monthly). This model mirrors the successful JWO camera based store launch process. 

Approach
We simplified and standardized deployment while reducing onsite staffing from 3-5+ blue badge personnel to an efficient 3-person yellow badge model (1 virtual PM, 2 onsite technicians per 3 lane store). A phased training ramp-up protects customer experience and product satisfaction. Key improvements include:

1. Semi-automated workflow (The Knowledge Scrolls, or TKS) 
2. Standardized deployment procedures
3. Target ~80% reduction in operational costs per installation 
    1. inclusive of E2E deployments for pop-ups where tear down is required

Requirements and Objectives

This training represents ‘phase 1’ of transitioning internally-owned launches to 3P TD for network setup, hardware deployment, and end-to-end testing. Metrics to be tracked include installation quality, time-to-completion, and first-time validation pass rates to meet 2026-2027 scaling demands alongside the ≥500% increase in JWO camera launches. Training Strategy

The program follows a structured 3-phase certification model designed to validate operational readiness while mitigating deployment risk and protecting the customer/operator experience. Phase 1 (Q4 2025) focused on shadow launch opportunities where vendor technicians observed and participated in a live deployment alongside IM/SSE teams. Phase 2 (Q1 2026) transitions to reverse shadow validation, where vendors lead deployments with LM/IM oversight to verify competency and identify gaps before full independence. Phase 3 (Q2 2026) certifies the vendor for independent scaling management, enabling them to meet quarterly demand without onsite blue badge support.

Training Audience and Expansion
1 vendor demonstrated the organizational agility required for rapid Q4-Q1 scaling through committed cross-training of 1 Project Manager (virtual) and 4 regionally distributed Lead Technicians spanning East Coast, West Coast, Central, and Southwest territories. This regional distribution ensures geographic coverage for simultaneous national deployments while minimizing travel costs and response times for permanent and pop-up launches. 

The selected technicians met critical prerequisites: possessed Amazon-provided laptops for TKS access and established experience with camera-based JWO launch processes, escalation protocols, and Amazon policies. This JWO foundation accelerated RFID training by leveraging existing operational knowledge and reducing onboarding friction. Additionally, each technician that trained onsite leads a regional team, creating a force multiplier effect where their certification enables downstream cross-training to support the 15+ simultaneous launches required in 2026 and beyond.

This training model validates both individual technical competency and organizational capacity to scale, ensuring 3P TD Vendor can transition from supervised deployments to independent operations while maintaining installation quality and first-time validation pass rates. 

This Q4 2025 pilot establishes the foundation for Q2 2026 vendor expansion

Success Criteria

Vendor certification requires demonstrated proficiency across five operational dimensions: 

1. technical understanding of RFID hardware and network architecture
2. process adherence to standardized deployment procedures
3. effective documentation usage through TKS workflows
4. independent troubleshooting capability without escalation
5. readiness to execute the ideal state 3P deployment end-to-end process

These criteria ensure vendors can operate independently while maintaining installation quality and first-time validation pass rates required for scaling.

Vendor Reported Pilot Gaps

Communication Gaps Identified

1. Pre-Launch Site Information - Vendor requested details (POC info, site access/badging, parking, start times) that weren't proactively provided. Techs arrived at an entry gate with no parking and POC phone went to voicemail, causing onsite delays.
2. Infrastructure Assumptions - Both stores ran on generators that lost power for 40 minutes (earthquake and high winds), hard-blocking progress. Vendor did not receive advance communication about generator-powered sites, backup power protocols, or site-specific power management.
3. System Access Permissions - Vendor identified that 3P vendors lack Mycroft camera stream access for validation, requiring escalation to LM. This creates dependency on blue badge personnel and blocks independent troubleshooting.
4. Missing Hardware Documentation - Credit card reader brackets were missing from shipment/lanes. Despite checking all boxes and confirming with operator team, the mounts weren't included, requiring workaround solution and follow-up ordering.
    1. Increased costs: 1) hot shot order and 2) revisit
5. TKS Field Visibility - Handover ticket fields were grayed out or missing descriptions (TC serial numbers, device manager alias), requiring mid-deployment clarification from LM.

Pilot Response Time Breakdown: 

Never Answered:  Vendor PM request for diagram/layout location at 8:15 AM PST on January 19 received no response in the conversation thread. 

Longest Hard Block Period: Vendor reported power loss at 3:27 PM PST. Vendor reported power restoration at 4:08 PM PST. Blocked window: 41 minutes; onsite team did not receive an acknowledgement of non-controllable circumstance from launch team during this time. 

Average Response Time During Onsite Deployment: 
Picture Requests - 2-13 minutes (vendor to LM)

Training Gaps Identified

1. Antenna Validation Troubleshooting - Gate 1 antenna validation failed multiple times. vendor PM attempted remote validation but encountered TKS bugs requiring refresh/multiple retries. Validation passed after multiple retry attempts, but no documented protocol for retry cadence or troubleshooting steps prompted in TKS. 
2. Physical Connection Diagnostics - Lane 2 errors required opening side panels to check gateway connections. Issue resolved after reseating cable on port 6 at gateway, but this troubleshooting step wasn't documented in TKS workflow.
3. Generator/Power Management - No documented protocols for generator-powered sites, power loss scenarios, or coordination with POC for generator restoration.
4. Crate Management Best Practices - LM noted that crates should remain assembled for easier re-crating during teardown. Technicians broke down crates due to site space constraints, but this wasn't covered in training or TKS documentation. Site-specific storage constraints require flexible crate management protocols, but need to be clearly described in TKS. 
5. Tamper Error Handling - Tamper errors appeared when panels were open. LM explained this was expected and could be cleared remotely after panels closed, but this wasn't documented in TKS workflow.
6. TC Device Management -  TC device manager alias issue remained unresolved throughout deployment. LM instructed team to skip TC tasks and leave devices in boxes, but no training or documentation clarified when TC tasks are required vs. optional. 
7. Receipt Validation Process - NA-AA-478 receipts didn't appear on receipt website despite transactions showing in Operator's bank account. LM explained this was related to camera issues, but no documented troubleshooting steps for receipt validation failures.
