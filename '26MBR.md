Feasibility Study  (Kushagra Srivastava, Xiaoqing Ge, Aaron McDaniel, Fred Savage)

We kicked off deep-dive last quarter to demonstrate real-time cart generation feasibility for RFID Fast Lanes with a goal to display priced carts within 4s. Here is the summary of progress made across 2 key workstreams through November and December in 2025:

1) Accuracy: We collected production representative data across different antenna configurations for the existing Gen-2.1 lane with minimal hardware changes (for faster launch in production) and “ideal” configuration (for reduced lane size, hardware cost). We demonstrated that the existing Gen-2.1 lane with minor accommodations (explained in next section) achieved total 29.3 EPK (2.93% error-rate) with 1.39 EPK (0.139% error-rate) due to zero RFID reads. For the “ideal” lane configuration, we demonstrated a total 25.87 EPK (2.587% error-rate) with 1.45 EPK (0.145% error-rate) due to zero RFID reads. These results were generated without retraining the algorithm with data and we expect to improve accuracy further as we collect more data. Simultaneously, Embedded team tuned a set of heuristics on the “ideal” lane datasets to demonstrate as low as 19.5 total EPK (1.95% error-rate). This gives us confidence that we can achieve <20 EPK (2% error-rate) for the Real-Time cart generation on Fast Lanes when we iterate with more data.

2) Latency: We conducted proof-of-concept experiments and architecture dive-deep to ensure that latency across RFID data transmission, algorithm inference, and pricing the cart can support the e2e 4s latency goal across on-prem and cloud. For on-prem, we determined that we can achieve on-prem data transmission, algorithm inference, and pricing calculation for fully integrated stores in few hundred-milliseconds. The pricing calculation for delegated stores would depend on our partner API latency and we expect that we will have to address this latency with an engaging user experience. We are still evaluating the latency opportunity for the cloud-hosted model. 

We plan to complete analysis by 2/8 and set the architecture strategy for low-latency cart generation. The next section describes how we are mobilizing our hardware team for a faster Gen-2.2 evolution to launch this in production.

Gen 2.2 implementation (Alex Xing Fred Savage)
Building RFID lane hardware for real-time cart is inherently difficult, and delivering it on an accelerated timeline on top of existing Gen 2.1 infrastructure is even harder. Gen 2.2 solves both problems simultaneously, delivering real-time cart capability pilot ready by 6/30. Deep Dive conducted by the team during feasibility study identified two critical elements that enable real-time cart: (1) moving barrier and auth/screen devices to lane-end for significantly improved shopper RFID zone coverage and (2) increasing antenna power by +3dBm for maximized RFID reads. Armed with these insights, the team invented and simplified, concluding a Gen 2.2 design that implements both elements while keeping most internal components in place. Gen 2.2 design reverses shopper flow, turning the entry zone into an exit zone, which naturally positions auth/screen devices close to lane-end. Combined with shelf adjustments and barrier relocation to align with lane-end, plus ADA-compliant screens for shopper/associate interaction, we see this design will achieve real-time cart goal (98% item-level accuracy, ≤4 sec latency, by the 6/30).

The goals of the Store Configuration & Launch (SCL) workstream are to be able to: (1) Increase launch capacity to 3 stores/wk (Q1) ramping to 7 stores/wk (Q3) [currently 1 store/wk], (2) Launch stores within 5 weeks of Order Form signing [current average 9.6w], and (3) Launch stores with < 26 person-hours of effort : <10 pre-launch [currently >34ph] and < 16 launch day (currently > 24ph), and fewer than 3 tickets (1 Sev2) per launch [currently > 8 tickets (2 Sev2s)].  To achieve these goals the team has completed defining and baselining the SCL-related metrics we will track throughout 2026, has finalized the 2026 SCL process design, and is in final implementation and validation of a new Store Creation and Configuration process that scales and shortens store configuration steps to <3w, and a new on-site launch support model using scalable 3P virtual and on-site resources (TechNet).  The on-site launch support model was verified at the Hard Rock Stadium retrofit, validated at the Kennedy Space Center launch, and is ready for all future 2026 launches.  Moving to a 3P launch support model will have initially small cost efficiencies that will increase as store launches increase, with the true value being the unburdening of tech blue badges to focus on development and innovation.  The new SCL processes are being validated first with the Chili Peppers Baseball opportunity (launch ETA 3/19) and will be finalized in the next 1 or 2 new customer opportunities in Q1.

Appendix B: NRF Learnings

NRF update (Nitin Khanna) NRF visit validated demand for XCO across major apparel retailers (Lululemon, Gap, Under Armor, Carter's, Uniqlo, Nordstroms and REI), Shoe manufacturers (Nike, Deckers (Hoka, Ugg)) and other interested solution providers like Fujitsu, Toshiba) NRF visit confirms our strategy to target retailers who source-tag their inventory 

    1. Key Insights: Retailers like Lululemon, Carters and Gap prefer associate-led and flat surface designs over bin-based self-checkout, Old Navy and Carter’s liked Bin based self checkout for their outlet mall location. Our multimodal fusion capability (camera + RFID) was a major differentiator that addresses missing tag detection and accuracy concerns, that competitors cannot solve. SAP and Oracle (key ERP providers) are not developing AI agents for store-level demand data integration into forecasting models—creating opportunity for our differentiation.
    2. Value Drivers: 
        1. Returns validation is important to retailers (Lululemon, Under Armor, Gap, Nordstrom, Hoka), with immediate value seen in RFID-based return authentication to reduce fraud. 
        2. Labor efficiency is critical—Under Armor explicitly stated labor is their "second largest expense" with shortage being a critical problem. 
        3. Missing tag detection via camera is highly valued, with Lululemon and Uniqlo reporting issues with tag removal fraud. 
    3. XCO Product priorities (1) product to include two SKUs an Associate led checkout with a flat surface RFID scanner and a Bin based self-checkout (2) returns validation workflow (3) Multimodal integration including RFID and Computer vision for accuracy improvements, (4) AI-agent for associate task prioritization and 
    4. Integration Requirements: Retailers expect simple deployment including fully delegated payment and loyalty system integration (Oracle POS, Shopify, retail cloud platforms), with backend integration to existing ERP, inventory management, and catalog/pricing systems as table stakes.  
    5. Competitor insights: 
        1. EXO has surface-based solutions with associate-assist models claiming faster checkout compared to self-checkout, contrary to Uniqlo's claim that RFID checkout is 2 times faster—likely indicating EXO's low accuracy requires more associate intervention. EXO's flat surface RFID checkout appeared to be proof-of-concept with visible accuracy issues. 
        2. RFID accuracy remains the primary concern, this was confirmed by direct feedback from Carter's (Piloted with Mishipay in one store and stopped due to accuracy and inability to integrate with Loyalty system) and Uniqlo (achieving 95%+ accuracy and experiencing stray tag detection issues).
