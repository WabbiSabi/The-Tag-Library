2) New Features

2.1 5G

One Sentence Summary: NA-AA-326 piloted 5G connectivity and showed it has potential as a connectivity solution but the following must be completed before offering to customers: 1) validate Vega lane network resiliency (jitter, packet loss, extreme network fluctuations, etc) and 2) create a rubric to indicate which store configurations should be considered for 5G connective to reduce customer and Amazon risk.

5G was the exclusive networking solution used for the NA-AA-326 launch in downtown Seattle (other solutions were unable to meet needs of store launch). 5G network speeds remained consistent (~150Mbps down and ~30Mbps up) and had no impact on shopper experience, CXQO workflows, or for any embedded functions. During the 8hr event there was a total of 30.6GB of data transferred through the 5G network with 6.2GB downloaded and 24.4GB uploaded for two lanes. The large downloads (~5GB) were due to Nemo cameras. See Appendix B: 5G Metrics for additional Peplink and Eero screenshots from the NA-AA-326 event.
At NA-AA-370, it was decided to not proceed with 5G network as the event sold over 100k tickets for the event. A 5G modem was brought to the venue for data collection in such an environment and despite utilizing a high priority SIM. Prior to the event, the 5G modem showed 57.4Mbps down and 28.4Mbps up which then plummeted to 1.15 Mbps up and 0.46Mbps down during the event, ~98% loss of network speed. In contrast, one of the on-site team members achieved 100 Mbps up/down during the event when using their pixel-9 phone which may be related to it having mmWave technology which our 5G modem does not. Even though 5G network was not reliable during the event, it did unblock store bring-up activities as the static WAN was not read in time to support on-site activities.

At NA-AA-90, an iPhone 10 had download speeds of 350Mbps and upload of 63Mbps during the Laufey concert. We were informed by Crypto.com IT that they had a state of the art Distributed Antenna System (DAS) recently installed on the roof of the venue which would ensure consistent network speeds despite a sold-out event. While at NA-AA-121, a cell phone experienced 25Mbps down and <5Mbps up when inside the venue and 20-160Mbps down and 1-44Mbps up when outside the venue.

This pilot program shows the potential opportunity that 5G presents for pop-up store locations but it is not generally available at this time. At a high level we still need to finalize: 1) Lab testing for poor network resiliency (packet loss, jitter, insufficient bandwidth), 2) finalize the hardware and assembly, and 3) a rubric to understand which store locations and opportunities are appropriate for 5G use.

2.2 Dynamic Pre-Auth (DPA)

One Sentence Summary: DPA implementation at NA-AA-330 so far resulted in 0% Fraud and Abuse (F&A) loss but is not generally available because 1) the feature has only been validated with IM30 credit card reader and 2) additional pilots are necessary in high throughput stores to validate customer experience and throughput. However, directional feedback from Brad S. (Rank & Rally) has been positive: “All feedback from shoppers (via our in-store ops team) indicates that DPA reduced shopper confusion”.

Dynamic Pre-Auth (DPA) was first launched at NA-AA-330 and was used for events on 9/20 and 9/27. The events saw 139 total transactions with 0% F&A loss. 90 of 139 transactions had no F&A risk because the pre-auth exceeded the final price, a 65% reduction to F&A risk. 49 of 139 transactions had F&A risk but at a lower exposure. The average cost for the 49 F&A risk transactions was $82.53 with DPA reducing 71% of F&A exposure by setting the pre-auth to $66.12 instead of using the $25 static pre-auth value. 

We were able to get feedback from Mywzsett, the store manager for NA-AA-330, while on-site for the 9/27 event. She highlighted two things: 1) customers were much less confused about the pricing displayed on the screen for DPA than for static pre-auth which made the associates explanation easier and 2) 100% reduction to F&A declines was a huge win. She also mentioned two opportunities for improvement: 1) the displayed amount is not the actual value and customers really wanted to see that loyalty discount had been applied and 2) the overall checkout experience felt like it took longer but considering their lower throughput numbers wasn’t “the worst thing”. 

Mywzsett’s comment regarding slower customer checkouts is worth further testing (or pilots) to understand as NA-AA-330 is a poor candidate to examine impact on throughput (only 159 transactions in 2 events). During the 9/20 event we did record gate dwell time (GDT) metrics with an average GDT was 23.6s, median GDT was 21.7s and P90 GDT was 27.6s. Unfortunately we are unable to understand GDT impact due to DPA because beam break instrumentation was incorporated into the DPA release and was not available for previous events.

3) What Went Well

3.1 Teardown Of Store Took Less Than 2hrs At All Stores

One Sentence Summary: Store teardowns took less than 2hrs largely due to AEG’s Stream at Authentication feature; opportunities are available to reduce teardown time further and simplify in preparation to hand off to 3P resources.

Post event teardown took less than or equal to 2 hours at all four pop-up locations. That time includes the virtual decommissioning of network, cameras, and compute devices as well as the lane preparation for shipment to include lowering of the arm, disassembly, and crating. Fast teardowns was made possible by AEG who released the Stream at Authentication feature. Stream at Authentication works by grabbing the Nemo video stream from 3 minutes before and after authentication and pushing that to the server for viewing, as opposed to users requesting footage directly from Nemo. This allows CXQO to view Nemo stream footage even when the lane is offline. This reduces the original 3hr SLA waiting period down to less than or equal to 10min (to account for any spooling), a 94% reduction. For pop-ups this has a direct impact as customers want us to close our operations as fast as possible to reduce access fees assessed by length of stay. This also impacts future customers looking to leverage tour mode or mobility mode as they will want to move lanes as soon as possible. Stream at Authentication 

The 2hr teardowns were performed by on-site blue badge employees that were familiar with the overall process and indicates the lower limit of time to complete. Virtual simplification is possible by incorporating the teardown workflow into TKS which would allow us to replace the manual MCM process with an automated workflow that can adapt to the store configuration. Physical simplification is possible by creation of a mechanism to retain fasteners, loose hardware, and spares for easy storage and to make it obvious when something is missing; this will also ensure consistent out of crate experience for future store locations.

3.2 Initial Vega Support Mechanisms Are Already Providing Value

One Sentence Summary: OE tools are limited for Vega but efforts have started in the form of Tamper Reset and Benny Reboot via TKS and initial Nemo and Eero troubleshooting via TOC runbook Wiki.

Casey Wojcik and Chandan Kumar Gupta have started putting together aVega TOC runbook Wikithat has troubleshooting practices specific to Vega. The coverage is low but constantly evolving as we work towards providing appropriate training to support teams for break-fix failures. At NA-AA-326 we experienced a Nemo DSA failure which was recovered by TOC on-call using the wiki to navigate Eero Insight Dashboard and perform device reboot.

Additionally, Abhishek Lolage and Nate McIlvenna worked together to create a TKS tool that allows for users with appropriate permissions (encapsulates all of ARGON and Tech Launch Operations [TOC/EFS/TD]) to perform Tamper Reset and Benny reboot functions from a dashboard. This saves significant time over the previous process which required acquiring DeviceIDs and navigating through SSM docs that only embedded team members had access to before. This tool will be further refined and added to the Vega TOC runbook as well as Vega Bring-up Workflows to reduce strain on Embedded team resources.

3.4 Go/No-Go Decision Meetings Drove Quality Feature Pilots

Go/No-Go decision meetings were held for 5Gand for DPA pilots. The meetings were used to provide a no-go, conditional go, or go response from a range of cross-functional teams prior to launch. Owners and stakeholders were able to voice concerns and align on the risks and benefits of the features. This mechanism should continue being used for large features as it drove high quality pilots by providing a mechanism to align on launch blockers and also proved capable at preventing pilots when the risk was too great.

* DPA go/no-go meeting identified 4 launch blockers that included QA testing, customer education needs regarding catalog/price risks, UI/UX issues, and need to sanity check device-to-catalog endpoint APIs in prod. The escalation and closure of these items ensured that DPA launch was a success for Amazon and the customer! 
* 5G go/no-go meeting outcomes provided NA-AA-326 with a go designation and NA-AA-370 with a no-go designation. Both decisions were the appropriate responses as NA-AA-326 operated solely from 5G while NA-AA-370 experienced significantly diminished 5G speeds that would have resulted in the store being non-functional.

All of the September store launches experienced obstacles with the shipping process. One shipment of 2 lanes to the UK was initially blocked due to shipping paperwork and resulted in us excusing ourselves from a tradeshow opportunity with Levy [cost to ship: $5,202]. 4.2 Suboptimal Store Design Incurs Additional Cost or Limits Throughput

One Sentence Summary: We need to create a store design mechanism to optimize throughput so that 1) we do not incur additional costs associated with providing too many Vega lanes and 2) we do not under deliver on customer throughput by providing too few Vega lanes.

We have provided an suboptimal checkout solution at every pop-up store launch to date. Overplanning the number of lanes is costly as it relates to the price to transport additional material but also because we then limit lanes to support additional activations. Insufficient lanes limits our ability to reduce shopper wait time (lines) and customers eagerness to seek additional activations (profit). In August and September we estimated the number of lanes for a store based on the customers expectation of shopper turnout, rife with its own bias, and did not consider the size of the space or the checkout experience. When a lane is fully utilized (has a line) we can process a maximum of ~170 transactions per hour when using Adyen with DPA disabled. Unfortunately, the decision is not as easy as selecting the appropriate number of lanes to meet the expected number of shoppers for the active shopping time. This is mainly due to constraints of the store size (tents, or constricted spaces) which has restricted customer flow into the store and prevented achieving the max throughput in 4 of the last 5 launches. NA-AA-90 was the only store that did not have enough lanes and it inhibited our ability to meet shopper quantities (lanes had lines for the majority of the time that artist was not performing). These lessons should be provided to future customers in form of store design recommendations which will allow customers to optimize customers and highlight the benefits provided by Vega.

However it was also observed that shoppers were frequently using Mobile Wallet and the lack of explanation could be the reason for the negative feedback.

Confirmation of this confusion was seen later that night at NA-AA-90 when a shopper returned to the store because their final cart included items they had not purchased. When using the EPC RLW they were coincidentally provided with a receipt containing Zach Bryan merchandise (NA-AA-370). 4.4 Detection, Segmentation, and Association (DSA) Tracking Failures Prevented CXQO Processes

One Sentence Summary: A new DSA requirement means that when the service is unhealthy that Mycroft streams are not viewable and prevents CXQO from performing session reviews. 

Detection, Segmentation, and Association (DSA) Tracking is a service that runs on the Nemo camera and is required to appropriately obfuscate RGB streams (as well as other JWO functions that Vega does not use). At the end of August, DSA streams became a requirement in order to view Mycroft streams such that when DSA is unhealthy, the Mycroft stream is completely obfuscated such that they are usable for CXQO. This was done to meet obfuscation and security requirements across JWO. 

Previously, we disregarded DSA failures on Nemo as they did not affect the operation of Nemo or CXQO workflows. However, since the requirement has been put into place we have experienced two issues with DSA. At NA-AA-326 (LSF SEA) DSA service went down during store open hours and we were unable to answer 20 LC shopping sessions during downtime which did not recover after restarting the service. At NA-AA-90 (Laufey LA) a DSA error was experienced during bring-up which TOC runbooks were unable to fix. Escalation to STOP was needed to mitigate the issue. 

This failures kicked off a STOP team RCA which uncovered that the Vega use case for DSA is unsupported. In JWO stores, Nemo cameras are built with overlap such that downtime on one Nemo does not have serious implications for overall store functionality. Vega is designed with a single Nemo camera per lane which means downtime on one Nemo results in complete loss of CXQO workflows during outages.
See action items in: Nemo DSA Resiliency

4.5 Adyen with Amazon Merchant ID (MID) is a Quick Fix for Customers but at Reduced Functionality

One Sentence Summary: Using the Amazon MID with Adyen offers customers an option to meet payments readiness for short timeline launches but comes at additional cost to Amazon teams to settle payments and lack of real-time metrics for the customer.

Customers are wanting to launch stores with truncated timelines which we have seen cause complications for many stores where they are unable to acquire a their MID in time to meet store launch timeline. We have unblocked customers by using Adyen P400 credit card readers with Amazon MID to collect payment and then distribute funds after the event is completed. This has been a great mechanism to launch stores but has also caused churn internally and with our customers. 

All customers (Amazon Music, Brand Like That, Rank and Rally, and Happy Belly) for the September stores used Adyen credit card readers with Amazon Mid and complained about their inability to monitor real-time transaction data. On-site staff were frequently asked to provide transaction counts as well as total sales metrics. Because there were multiple stores operating in this configuration, we had to segregate real-time data based Adyen credit card reader serial number (SN) which mandated accurate tracking against location and date for each card reader. At the end of the event, customers want their money and they want it now, we have received multiple escalations from customers to fast track fund transfers. This is further complicated by there being insufficient funds from the events to cover the total sales owed to customers due to F&A losses. Most of these concerns should be mitigated with launch of FI using the Wisepad BBPOS targeted for EOY 2025.

Internally, Amazon Adyen account did not support Discover card transactions because Discover did not support incremental authentications (the process by which additional charges are applied against a credit card transaction to cover the difference between pre-auth and final cost). At NA-AA-90 and NA-AA-370, Discover card purchases were encountered much more frequently (anecdotally) which upset customers who had spent up to an hour in line waiting to make a purchase only to be turn away or forced to use a non-preferred credit card.

4.6 Escalations at Store Installation and Bring-up

One Sentence Summary: Vega is averaging >12 sev2s per launch causing excessive escalation to on-calls and support teams. Prioritization is needed to: 1) ensure services are ready in time for bring-up and 2) ensure Vega lanes have an appropriate post-event ‘wipe’ process so that subsequent launches are hassle-free.

With four launches in one month, September added considerable strain to our internal teams as we saw more than 50 sev2s triggered during bring-up. Escalations for Vega are generally more impactful than similar issues with a JWO store as Vega operates on a truncated launch timeline spanning 1-3 days prior to launch day. The number of sev2s is considerably less than the true number of escalations as many issues were managed with dev teams via slack when urgency was paramount. Issues encountered are explained in greater detail in 5) Summary of Issues section below but it’s important to note that this level of escalation has an impact for dev teams and especially their on-calls as they are supporting all JWO stores in addition to Vega. Scalability requires that we are capable of launching stores without mandatory dev team support.

The two sources of escalations that caused the most bring-up churn across the store launches can be isolated to two main themes: 

* Service are not brought up in time for TD on-site: At NA-AA-121, NA-AA-326, NA-AA-370 Elixir was not showing sessions appropriately because Astra and Brahmos services were not ready and NA-AA-326 was also delayed because of a cloud config change that did not get pushed to prod in time. 
* Untidy exchange of Vega lanes: At NA-AA-326, NA-AA-90, and NA-AA-370 we faced hours of delays during bring-up because Vega lanes persisted old gate information. At NA-AA-326 this was seen by both ee.gate.1 and ee.gate.2 identifying as ee.gate.1 (ee.gate.2 has previously been ee.gate.1 of NA-AA-121 and it stuck). At NA-AA-90 it showed as both ee.gate.1 and ee.gate.2 persisting as ee.gate.3 and ee.gate.4 which caused Auth wavefront to lag and excessive Brahmos, triggering sev2 escalation. NA-AA-370 had a similar issue where the lane had been temporarily provisioned to a gamma lab (to perform gate blade troubleshooting) and persisted the lane information.

4.7 On-Site Launch Support Still Needed

One Sentence Summary: On-site Amazonian presence is still needed until we have a process that has less problems and has better mechanism for identification when problems are encountered.

As part of the last Vega retrospective (NA-AA-388 Retro) we identified that on-site support was needed in order to maintain a high quality experience for customers and to manage on-site resiliency concerns. This recommendation is still in place. On-Site teams were still necessary to support bring-up. As noted at NA-AA-90 by the 3PTD, “the physical install was pretty easy, as well as TKS, but I wouldn’t have known how to deal with any of the escalations”. This suggests there is an opportunity to become more launch scalable (reduce on-site presence) by deep diving into the bring-up process to: 1) create a more robust bring-up process that reduces the occurrence of failure and 2) augment the TKS workflow to better identify failures, provide self-serve resolution mechanism, and when that doesn’t work provide accurate escalation paths. We should identify a path to launch a store within our 2-day SLA using 3PTD resources with a pilot in Q4.

4.8 Associate Training  at Pop-Ups Needs Additional Refinement

One Sentence Summary: Amazonians provided QR-based instructions were not well-received at NA-AA-90 and show that training by Amazon Ambassadors, while not scalable, provides the best associate outcomes to date.

Amazon presence at store launches led to significant on-the job training (OJT). At NA-AA-90, we tried to be very specific in providing the new training material which is a laminated page with QRs taking associates can use to look up Vega information. When instructed by Amazon to user their phone to scan the QRs, the associate manager stated that employees are not able to use their phone while working for any reason. After convincing associates to use training material, the associates did not read the provided material with one person stating  were not interested in reading the material “I gotta be honest with you, i was really expecting a video”. Multiple attempts to train on-site associates have been attempted but the results from OJT (Amazon Ambassadors) continues to provide the best results, unfortunately, this methodology is not scalable.

Future possibilities include tablets which are could host the training videos on them. This removes the access issue onsite and presents the material in a manner that the user expects (straight video with a reading option via the linked guide). The tablets could be become part of BOM and returned for pop-ups. This solution needs alignment before piloting but shouldn’t pose much pushback as JWO looks to implement Bring Your Own Device (BYOD)

Appendix A: Priority Rubric

Blast Radius

* All stores: An issue that is likely to be encountered at every single store launch
* Most Stores: An issue that is configuration dependent and will occur at equal to or more than half of store launches
* Some Stores: An issue that is configuration dependent and will occur at less than half of store launches
* Limited: An issue that we may see again but is unlikely. This also includes renovations.

Severity

* Blocker: An issue that must be fixed. Loss of critical feature. Likely to impact on-time launch without heroics.
* High: An issue that should be fixed. Loss of non-critical feature or impaired critical feature.
* Medium: An issue that can be managed. Workaround is possible with adequate preparation.
* Low: An issue with minimal impact. Can be ignored or quickly resolved
