[EU/UK] Pre-Poured Beverages Program

Pre-poured beverages represent a $TK business and a $TK growth opportunity for the RFID program.  UK football stadiums restrict alcohol sales to before matches and at halftime creating short periods with extremely high demand.  Sales velocity is the highest priority for shopper experience and sales revenue.  Alcohol retailers have partially addressed the high demand by pre-pouring beer to reduce the sales transaction duration to just checkout; however, long lines persist with traditional POS stations. Amazon’s RFID lanes have demonstrated the ability to create fast, frictionless shopping experiences with checkout times as low as 15sec, TK% faster than current pre-pour beer sales cycle times.  Adding pre-poured beverages to RFID Lanes’ supported selection could further shorten lines and increase revenues, but would require additional JWO efforts to identify RFID-enabled cups that ensure tag readability, durability, and tamper resistance, while meeting cost and aesthetic expectations.  

Standard pint pour takes 7-8 sec while a traditional POS checkout taking ~30sec in an ideal situation, an average poured-beverage experience from tap to POS takes almost a minute or two at the first available POS station.
By utilizing pre-pour this would mean your average setup will cut down on the 8sec it takes to wait for a pour, but not expedite the actual time suck which would be waiting for POS checkout of the customer in front of you in order to order a beverage and checkout yourself.


RFID Solutions

One of two primary challenges in RFID-enabling pre-pour beverages, and beverages in general, is that RF energy is absorbed by liquids and reflected by metals commonly used in beverage containers.  RFID tag designers have been able to improve tag readability through improvements to the tag inlay (I.e. antenna) design and maintaining as little as 4mm physical separation between the tag and the liquid or metal interfaces using plastic and foam spacers. Although tag readability is improved, these tags still do not perform as well as tags in free space and still require consistent line of sight from nearby RF antennas to maintain readability. As a preliminary unlock and a pilot to understand the operational constrains for RFID tagging beverages,  Vega has recently piloted our beverage program at PGA, F1, and recently at LSU with Midas flag-tags.


The second challenge is the immaturity of the RFID industry in manufacturing and operationalizing RFID-enabled beverage solutions. RFID tags add $0.05-0.10 to the manufacturing cost of each disposable cup ($0.007-0.0125, ref) preventing manufacturers from developing scalable manufacturing processes and making it RFID-enabled cups a formal product offering without a high-demand customer.  Customers are similarly apprehensive to incur the higher cup cost without clear offsetting operational or sales improvements.  The integration effort and profitability of a RFID pre-pour beverage solution improves with greater vertical integration.

Several companies in the UK and EU are developing reusable container solutions, some co-piloting with global brands like McDonald’s and Starbucks; however, they often lack the retail integration platforms and scalability JWO can offer. With our RFID technology and relationships, JWO is uniquely positioned to bridge the gap between innovation and mass adoption in a way McDonalds and Starbucks are limited to in their respective categories.
