RFID Tagging Integration into the Vega Store Launch Workflow

Purpose

Amazonian-led tagging at $50-$75/hr does not scale to 150 launches in 2026 and 450 in 2027. Pilot data showed a 3.43% non-payment loss rate tied directly to tag placement accuracy, and MerchOps spend the majority of on-site time on tagging rather than technical work. This document outlines where and why 3P vendor tagging ($35-$52/hr, 30-53% cost reduction) is being inserted into the Vega store launch workflow as a parallel workstream.


Where Tagging Fits in the Workflow

Step 2: SCOT Intake and Tagging Feasibility (New additions to existing step)

When the Implementation Manager creates the SCOT intake SIM, two new parallel activities are triggered:

- BD submits an intake to MerchOps containing catalog selection, merchandise count, item types, packaging details, historical sales data, location logistics, and the operational plan.
- MerchOps performs a catalog and technology evaluation — reviewing item materials, packaging types, and form factors against validated tag-merchandise combinations, flagging bespoke tagging needs (metallic packaging, liquids, irregular shapes), and confirming tag type per merchandise category (M8 sticker, Midas flag-tag, hang-tag, etc.).
- MerchOps outputs a tagging feasibility assessment that informs tier classification and vendor scoping downstream.

This is inserted at Step 2 because tier classification, vendor selection, and pre-print decisions all depend on the feasibility output. Starting this evaluation any later would create a bottleneck — catalog and technology gaps discovered after vendor engagement or material shipment would delay launches and erode the cost savings the program is designed to deliver.

Step 3: RFID Tagging Workstream (New parallel track alongside existing workstreams)

Once the feasibility assessment is complete, a new RFID tagging workstream runs in parallel with the existing Step 3 activities (BOM shipment, 3PTD allocation, WAN/network setup, StoreID assignment, payment/Libby configuration):

1. Tier classification determined (Tier 1/2/3) based on MerchOps feasibility assessment, venue logistics, and merchant capacity
2. Service model selected (Model A: Full-Event Tagging, Model B: Launch-Day Tagging, or Model C: Auditing Presence) based on tier and merchant preference
3. Tagging quote generated and provided to merchant
4. Merchant approves quote and vendor engagement is confirmed
5. Customer site(s) visited and logistics evaluated
6. Pre-print fulfillment initiated (Tier 2/3) or on-site printing confirmed with consumables added to BOM
7. Vendor receives SOPs

This workstream converges with the existing TKS-guided execution at Step 4 (Virtual Bring-Up and Validation), where vendor on-site tagging operations run alongside hardware validation before Launch.


Why This Placement

Starting tagging conversations at Step 2 and executing as a Step 3 parallel workstream is driven by three factors from pilot experience:

1. Catalog variance requires early evaluation. Pilot events showed merchant tag estimates consistently underproject actual need by 45-200% (PGA-AMEX: 7,000 estimated vs. ~17,000 actual). MerchOps needs time to identify gaps, flag bespoke solutions, and determine whether pre-printing or on-site printing is appropriate before materials ship.

2. Vendor lead time is real. Action Link requires 6-8 weeks staffing notice. WIS International schedules a free on-site visit 7-14 days before the project date. These timelines only work if vendor engagement is confirmed during Step 3 — not after hardware arrives on-site.

3. Tagging and hardware deployment are independent workstreams. Tagging operations (catalog validation, tag printing, vendor allocation) have no dependency on hardware uncrating or virtual bring-up. Running them in parallel avoids extending the overall launch timeline while ensuring tagged merchandise is ready when gates go live.


Financial Impact

Transitioning tagging to 3P vendors across the projected 150 store launches in 2026 represents a total financial impact of greater than $226,000 or 1,200 hours of effort. Per-event vendor costs range from $70-$1,248 depending on tier and vendor, compared to $2,300-$3,300+ fully-loaded cost per event using Amazonians (including T&E and opportunity cost).
